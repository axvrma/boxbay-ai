# Optimization and explanation

FIFO uses a deterministic first feasible slot and queue order. Legacy tiering applies the old dwell-based 1/2/3 placement preference and estimated energy within the same safety and execution model. Reactive and predictive share the same CP-SAT approach. Predictive placement adds a possible retrieval cost and pressure penalty for allocating scarce near-interface or powered slots to lower-value uses. Forecast updates use known vessel notices and the corresponding rolling-origin monthly estimate, with a historical seasonal profile for unsupported months. Appointment inputs and the dwell model come from information available at dispatch.

Each candidate has selection, optional interval and start decisions. Constraints require no overlap for the dedicated crane and rack. The shared tray route is reserved separately for the two tray legs. Candidate scores include tray time, crane time and energy. Precedence requires committed storage before pickup. Hard cutoffs exclude missed-start alternatives. Same-commit hazard segregation is verified before each dispatch. The objective rewards service and queue age and penalizes start delay, projected tardiness, energy and travel. The model uses weighted second equivalents, with a configurable kWh-to-cost coefficient.

The planner commits immediate starts and replans after state changes. Delayed starts are provisional. Busy-resource release times enter the interval model. Single-worker CP-SAT uses a fixed deterministic search allowance and a deterministic feasible greedy fallback. Candidate pruning and estimated future crane positions make the result approximate. Optional relocation is restricted to one rack and compares pickup-readiness cost with doing nothing. The tray network routes inbound loads across rack lanes and returns outbound loads to the interface. Reviews may choose zero relocation.
